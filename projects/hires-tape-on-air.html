The problem

Initially, getting a clip out of compliance material meant low resolution overlaid with date and timestamp for recording, but not used for repurposing.

The build

Two pieces. A configurator that generates the recorder scripts, and a playback server that makes the resulting archive workable.

  • Configurator generating capture scripts for SDI, UDP, RTSP and SRT channels, with user-defined presets replacing hand-editing
  • Playback server providing preview, trim, merge and archive mirroring over the browser
  • Separate paths for preview and for scrubbing, because the two have different demands
  • Low-resolution scrub proxies generated when material is mirrored, so scrubbing is responsive without touching the original
  • Exports delivered to a named server-side location, falling back to browser download

Getting frame accuracy right

This was the part worth building twice. The first approach recorded a high-resolution file and a low-resolution proxy as two parallel captures, on the assumption they would stay aligned. They did not — two independently encoded live streams drift, and production logs confirmed segment boundaries separating by several seconds. That approach was retired rather than patched, because no amount of correction fixes an architecture that assumes something untrue.

Proxies are now generated server-side from the mirrored original, which makes alignment a property of the process instead of a hope. Even then a residual misalignment of one to two frames survived, traced to timestamps being rebased during proxy generation rather than carried through. Preserving the original timestamps closed it.

One or two frames sounds like nothing. On a trim point it is the difference between a clean cut and a visible one.

The outcome

Aired material became reusable. A clip that needed a manual retrieval and a careful hand-cut is now found, scrubbed and exported in the browser, accurate to the frame.
