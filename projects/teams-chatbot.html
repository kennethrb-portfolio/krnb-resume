This service takes the live schedule out of Etere and publishes it as a self-refreshing page anyone with access can open — plus the scheduled reports the operation runs on.

The problem

Schedule information was locked behind an automation client. Partner channels outside the organization who needed to know what was on, what was next, or what ran yesterday had to ask a TOC operator, which interrupted the operator and delayed the person asking. A client needs a license to access the reports inside the automation, which entails costs. Reports were generated manually on a periodic basis.

The build

A Node service pulls each channel's playlist from Etere and renders it as an ordinary web page, published where the rest of the organisation already works. Delivered as an HTML file in a MS Sharepoint site every 2 minutes to ensure that the playlist being monitored stay fresh

  • Three days on one page — yesterday, today and tomorrow — refreshing in place rather than reloading and losing the reader's position
  • An on-air card driven by live playout status, with a running overrun counter
  • Polling that accelerates during a live overrun, when the schedule is moving fastest and accuracy matters most
  • Hourly PDFs with per-output retention, plus segmented production guides and daily runs generated on schedule
  • Per-channel configuration, so each channel independently controls which outputs it produces
  • Admin dashboard behind directory authentication, with manual generation for operators
  • Delivery of reports done over Microsoft Teams Group Chat and over e-mail via Power Automate, driven by configured triggers as per clients preferences.

Staying up when a server doesn't

A schedule page nobody trusts is a page nobody uses, so availability was treated as a feature rather than an afterthought. Two servers coordinate through a shared lease file with a fixed-primary policy: the standby takes over when the primary stops renewing, and hands back deliberately rather than fighting over ownership.

The failure modes that actually bit were subtler than a server going down — a fetch that hung forever instead of failing, and an authentication renewal that stopped pushes permanently once it lapsed. Both are now bounded by timeouts and watched by a supervisor that can restart the service on its own.

The outcome

Schedule information and menial tasks are handled automatically. TOC operator and MCR stopped fielding questions it had to stop work to answer, and the reports that used to depend on someone now arrive on their own. Services moved towards automated and self-service, effectively raising TOC operators productivity and focus on tasks that matter more. Furthermore, it eliminated the need to purchase licenses per user to access the services and reports.
